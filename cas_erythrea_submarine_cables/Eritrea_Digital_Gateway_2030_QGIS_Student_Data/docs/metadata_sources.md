# Metadata and Source Notes

## Public contextual data

Natural Earth low-resolution country polygons are used for country context. Natural Earth data are public domain and are suitable for small-scale maps. These geometries are generalized and are not appropriate for cadastral, legal, operational, or engineering analysis.

The seed internet-use values for Eritrea are reproduced from the Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis FRED series `ITNETUSERP2ERI`, whose source is the World Bank World Development Indicators. FRED reports 20.01040 internet users per 100 people for Eritrea in 2023, with the series updated on October 8, 2025. The World Bank identifies the underlying source as the International Telecommunication Union and World Bank estimates.

The global and regional comparison values in `internet_access_seed_indicators.csv` are taken from ITU Facts and Figures 2024. ITU reports that 5.5 billion people, or 68 percent of the world's population, were online in 2024; it also reports Africa's average internet use at 38 percent and low-income countries at 27 percent.

## Pedagogical layers

The following layers are synthetic and were produced solely for case-method teaching: scenario routes, submarine branches, schematic Red Sea cable corridors, satellite sites and buffers, risk zones, governance tables, and scenario scorecards. They are plausible only at the level of classroom reasoning. They are not official network data, they are not TeleGeography cable coordinates, and they must not be used for maritime planning, infrastructure procurement, security assessment, or public policy without replacement by validated data.

## Suggested replacement sources for empirical projects

Students can replace or supplement these classroom layers with data from the World Bank WDI API, ITU DataHub, Natural Earth, OpenStreetMap, TeleGeography's Submarine Cable Map for visual reference, ACLED under its licensing terms, and national telecom regulatory documents where available.
